# Section 10.4: Glossary

## Key Terms and Definitions

This glossary provides definitions of key terms used throughout this book. These definitions are concise but capture the essential meaning of each term. For more detailed explanations, refer to the relevant chapters where these terms are discussed in depth.

The glossary is organized alphabetically for easy reference. Some terms have multiple meanings depending on context, and the definitions provided here focus on their usage in materials science and machine learning applications. When encountering terms in the literature, be aware that definitions may vary slightly between sources, and context is important for understanding precise meaning.

This glossary is not exhaustive—the field has many specialized terms, and new terminology emerges as the field evolves. However, it covers the most important terms used throughout this book and provides a foundation for understanding the literature. For terms not covered here, consult specialized texts, review articles, or domain-specific resources.

### A

**Active Learning**: Machine learning approach that uses uncertainty estimates to intelligently select which samples to label or compute, reducing required data.

**Aleatoric Uncertainty**: Uncertainty due to inherent randomness or noise in data. Cannot be reduced with more data.

### B

**Band Gap**: Energy difference between valence and conduction bands in a semiconductor or insulator. Determines electronic properties.

**Basis Set**: Set of functions used to represent electronic wavefunctions in quantum mechanical calculations.

### C

**Cross-Validation**: Method for assessing model performance by dividing data into folds, training on some folds and validating on others.

**Crystal Graph**: Representation of crystal structure as a graph, where atoms are nodes and bonds are edges.

### D

**Density Functional Theory (DFT)**: Quantum mechanical method for computing electronic structure and properties of materials.

**Descriptor**: Numerical representation of a material that captures information relevant for property prediction.

### E

**Epistemic Uncertainty**: Uncertainty due to limited knowledge or data. Can be reduced by collecting more data.

**Exchange-Correlation Functional**: Approximate function in DFT that accounts for quantum mechanical exchange and correlation effects.

### F

**Formation Energy**: Energy required to form a compound from its constituent elements. Determines thermodynamic stability.

**Foundation Model**: Large pre-trained model that can be adapted to many different tasks with minimal additional training.

### G

**Graph Neural Network (GNN)**: Neural network architecture designed to work with graph-structured data, naturally suited for crystal structures.

**Gaussian Process Regression**: Probabilistic machine learning method that provides uncertainty estimates along with predictions.

### H

**High-Throughput Computing**: Systematic, automated approach to running many calculations, enabling exploration of large material spaces.

**Hybrid Functional**: DFT functional that mixes exact exchange with approximate exchange-correlation, often more accurate than pure functionals.

### I

**Inverse Design**: Approach that starts with desired properties and finds materials that might have them, rather than predicting properties of known materials.

### K

**K-Point Sampling**: Sampling of reciprocal space used in periodic boundary condition calculations to compute Brillouin zone integrals.

### M

**Materials Project**: Large database of computed materials properties, widely used for training ML models and materials discovery.

**Multi-Objective Optimization**: Optimization approach that simultaneously optimizes multiple competing objectives, revealing trade-offs.

### P

**Pareto Frontier**: Set of solutions where improving one objective requires worsening another. Represents optimal trade-offs in multi-objective optimization.

**Pseudopotential**: Approximation that replaces core electrons with an effective potential, reducing computational cost while maintaining accuracy.

### Q

**Quantum ESPRESSO**: Open-source software package for electronic structure calculations using plane waves and pseudopotentials.

### R

**Random Forest**: Ensemble machine learning method that combines many decision trees, robust and effective for many materials problems.

**Regularization**: Techniques to prevent overfitting by penalizing model complexity.

### S

**Self-Consistent Field (SCF)**: Iterative procedure in DFT to solve for electronic structure, where the solution must be consistent with itself.

**Surrogate Model**: Fast machine learning approximation of expensive calculations, enabling rapid screening.

### U

**Uncertainty Quantification**: Methods for estimating prediction uncertainty, enabling better decision-making and active learning.

### V

**VASP**: Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package, widely used commercial software for DFT calculations.

This glossary covers key terms, but many more exist. Consult specialized texts and resources for comprehensive definitions.
